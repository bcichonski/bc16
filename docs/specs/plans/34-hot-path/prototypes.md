## How each variant is built, and register/flag effects

- **`pp_rom`**: the private `poke16`/`peek16` bodies in `bc_stdlib.py` are replaced by stubs in the style of the other ROM stubs (`mov a, 0x09|0x0a`, `psh a`, `pop f`, `cal :os_metacall`, `ret`). All callers keep calling `:poke16`/`:peek16` (codegen, runtime-internal `clr`, and the B `asm "cal :peek16"` lines), so the B asm uses go through the stub too; no source text changes. Effects: `os_call` saves/restores a, csci, dsdi, then calls the ROM body with a = the index, so after the call a = 0x09/0x0a and f is overwritten (index, then the ROM body's flags). The documented contract already says `a - rubbish`; the runtime callers that need a (stackoffscalc, stackoffsclc8) push it before the call. The smoke/e2e run is the check that no B asm caller depends on a or f across the call.
- **`pp_inline`**: the bodies are removed from the runtime; every `cal :R`/`clr :R` line of the generated `.basm` (codegen, runtime-internal and B `asm` lines alike) becomes `mov cs, #dsdi / inc dsdi / mov ci, #dsdi` (peek16) or `mov #dsdi, cs / inc dsdi / mov #dsdi, ci` (poke16). Registers and flags at the end are exactly those of the private copy (the body is the same, minus `cal`/`ret`).
- **`cmp_copy`**: the three stubs are replaced by the ROM text of `gt16`, `gteq16`, `eq16` copied from `bcos.basm` (labels unchanged; the ROM `eq16` jumps to `gteq16_false`, kept as is). Result registers/flags are those of the ROM call minus the dispatch (a, flags set by the body; f is no longer the index on entry, which the bodies overwrite anyway).
- **`cmp_inline`**: the stubs are removed; every call line becomes the ROM body with labels renamed `pi<N>_<label>` (N unique per site), `eq16`'s `gteq16_false` mapped to its own `eq16_false`, and each inner `ret` replaced by a jump to `pi<N>_<name>_end`: after `mov a, 0x01` the jump is `jmr nz` (mov into a sets Z from the value, 1 -> Z clear), after `xor a` it is `jmr z`; a final `ret` is dropped. Relative jumps do not change flags, so a and all flags at the end are exactly those the ROM body returns (mseek's `clr :gteq16` / `jmr nz` relies on this).
- The inline post-pass runs in the temp copy's `bc.py` (`return proto_inline.transform(context.basm)`); that gives the same bytes as emitting the bodies in `bc_ast.py`, `bc_stdlib.py` and the B sources, and catches every origin in one place.
- Calling a ROM body by its direct address is excluded (D4): only `os_metacall`, `os_metatab` and `os_vartab` are stable.
